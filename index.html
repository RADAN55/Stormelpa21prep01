LESSONWatch the Coach: Mr. Brooks

Press Play lesson. Mr. Brooks explains the four parts of the test and his best tips.

TOURTry One Task From Each Domain

Work through all four cards. Read the Tip on each card before you answer.

Reading

Read a short text and answer

Tip: Read the question first. Then read the text looking for the answer.

Starting next Monday, the library will open at 7:15 a.m. instead of 7:45 a.m. Students can use the computers to finish homework before class. Food and drinks are still not allowed inside.

Listening

Listen and choose

Tip: Before you press Play, read the question so you know what to listen for.

Speaking

Answer out loud

Tip: Repeat the question in your first sentence. Keep talking until you give a reason and an example.

Prompt. Your friend is new at school. Tell your friend about one place at school that is important to know, and explain why.
Writing

Write about a picture

Tip: Write complete sentences. Tell who, what, and where. Then check capitals and periods.

TIPSSmart Move or Risky Move?

Sort each test-day choice. Read the coach’s reason after each one.

STRATEGYCross-Out Drill

Strong test takers cross out answers they know are wrong. Tap ✕ on two wrong answers first. Then the best answer unlocks.

Many teens say they cannot sleep without their phones nearby. However, doctors say the light from a screen tells the brain to stay awake. A study of high school students found that those who put their phones in another room fell asleep faster and felt less tired at school. Some students now use an old-style alarm clock so the phone can stay out of the bedroom.

GAMETest-Day Showdown

A quiz-show board with 15 questions in 5 categories. Pick any tile. Harder questions are worth more points. You get one chance per tile. Play with a partner and take turns choosing.

LESSONWatch the Coach: Ms. Patel

Press Play lesson. Ms. Patel shows the writing basics ELPA21 scorers look for.

STEP 1Sentence or Fragment?

A complete sentence needs a subject, a verb and a complete thought. Sort each one.

STEP 2Fix It

Each sentence has one or two mistakes (capital letter, end mark, verb, or a missing word). Type the corrected sentence.

STEP 3Stretch It

Short sentences get low scores. Make this sentence longer by answering when, where and why.

Start: Maria studied. → Example: After school on Tuesday, Maria studied in the library because she had a science test the next day.
